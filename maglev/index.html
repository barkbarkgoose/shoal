What the bench says

Phased push/pull pairs

Held still, every coil averages exactly zero over a cycle, so the total is zero at any stagger. At 90° the stagger works perfectly: at every instant the lifting units and the pushing-down units cancel to within 1% of the weight. Released, the platform falls in under 0.1 s. Adding DC bias gives real lift (0.89× the weight at bias 1), but that is a static magnet with ripple, and it tips over in 0.2 s.

Magnets only on board

Pairs mounted on the platform, however they oscillate, sum to zero force (the readout shows ~1e-16 N, which is rounding error). A platform cannot lift itself by pushing on itself. The lift has to react against the ground.

Static magnets

Permanent magnets carry the weight easily but slide off sideways in about 0.2 s. Lock sideways motion and tilt and it floats indefinitely. That one missing direction is what Earnshaw’s theorem guarantees, at any scale and with any number of magnets.

Found: pure AC can levitate

Coils with zero average current (half push, half pull) do hold a magnet up, stably, for as long as the sim runs. The platform shakes slightly in time with the drive and is always a little farther from the coils while they pull, so repulsion wins on average. Earnshaw only covers static fields, so this is allowed. It is the same physics as a Paul ion trap or Kapitza’s upside-down pendulum.

The twist on your idea

It only works when the units are in phase. Up to about 45° of stagger the four-trap platform stays up. At 90° it rocks and falls in 0.25 s, and at 180° it falls in 0.07 s. The platform has to jiggle as one body. Spreading the push and pull across units cancels the jiggle that produces the lift.

The cost

The AC platform needs coils peaking at 20 A·m² each: about twenty 1 cm neodymium cubes’ worth of moment, reversing 120 times a second. That means thousands of ampere-turns per coil and continuous resistive heating. Lift falls with frequency squared, and below about 60 Hz the trap goes unstable. It is a real effect but an expensive one, which is why trains use feedback control.

Model limits. Treat surprises here as leads to check, not as results.

  • Point dipoles with a 3–4 mm softening length. Real magnets have size, and forces at contact distance are approximate.
  • Motion is confined to one vertical plane: sideways, up/down, and one tilt. A real platform has three more ways to move (another sideways axis, two more tilts, spin), and each must also be stable.
  • Coils are ideal: current follows the drive instantly. No inductance, eddy currents, heating, or iron cores.
  • Semi-implicit Euler at 10–50 µs steps. The AC results are unchanged at 4 µs.
